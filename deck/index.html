CAN THE AI DO THIS ON ITS OWN?

Yes, within rules a designer sets.

01 · READ

It reads the message for its one concrete image and the phrase that carries it. In Iris’s letter: the porch light, still on and I noticed.

02 · CHOOSE

It picks a move from the mood’s own library: words circling a light, a phrase drawn inside the whole letter, a stack that gets heavier, a word set tiny.

03 · COMPOSE

It builds the cover from the message’s own words, paces the beats and cues the sound. It never adds, cuts or rewrites a word.

04 · SHOW

The sender sees it composed first. If it doesn’t land, a light nudge (warmer, quieter, simpler) recomposes it before it’s sealed.

The guardrails are the mood: its typefaces, palette, allowed moves, timing rules and sound palette. Taste stays with the designer.

SOUND IS HALF THE MESSAGE

A sound engine that listens to the words.

Each mood has its own palette of music and texture. The engine reads what’s written and places sound where the words and images land, so you feel a line before you’ve finished reading it.

WHY NOW, WHY US

Why now

AI can do
the choreography.

Pacing a message used to take a motion designer and a day. Now it takes seconds, inside rules a designer sets.

Messaging is fast and flat.

Everything lands in the same bubble, so a slower channel stands out.

Beautiful delivery
is already loved.

People keep designed invitations and cards long after the day.

Why us

A Kept message lives or dies on three things at once: type that feels set by hand, timing that feels like someone breathing, and sound that answers the words.

That isn’t a design job handed to an engineering job. The moods, the pacing, the sound and the player that performs them have to grow up together, argued over by two people who care about the same moment.

Templates are easy to copy. A voice, in type and in time, is not.
